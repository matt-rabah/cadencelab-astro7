# Cadence Lab voice guide

How every word on cadencelab.co should sound: pages, buttons, forms, error
messages, emails, and the FitCheck™ Decision Brief.

## The voice in one line

**Hard truths, gently told.**

Matt talks like a calm, experienced mentor who wants you to win and won't
let you fool yourself. Mostly Chris Do: direct, practical, Socratic, high
status without hype. Softened about one part in ten with Seth Godin: warm,
plain, a little philosophical, generous with white space.

Imagine each piece starts with "Here's what I'd tell a friend running this
team..." Delete that line before publishing. Keep the care it put there.

## Traits: this, not that

| We are | But never |
| --- | --- |
| Direct | Cold or curt |
| Confident | Salesy or boastful |
| Simple | Dumbed down |
| Warm | Soft, vague, or flattering |
| Honest about hard things | Harsh, smug, or shaming |
| Curious (we ask) | Interrogating or lecturing |
| Specific | Padded with jargon |

## How it sounds on the page

- **Lead with the point.** The first sentence carries the idea, a sharp
  question, or the hard truth. No warm-up.
- **Short lines, lots of air.** One to three sentences per paragraph. Mix
  short fragments with one explaining sentence.
- **Ask instead of tell.** Use questions to surface the real problem:
  "What's this costing you right now?" "What happens if nothing changes?"
- **Flip the cliché.** Take what everyone in CX says and turn it:
  "Your customers aren't difficult. Your handoffs are." Use one flip per
  piece, not one per paragraph.
- **Big claim, then proof.** Every bold statement is followed by a specific
  fact, figure, or example. Name the source for any statistic.
- **Talk to one person.** "You" and "I". Cadence Lab is Matt; write in the
  first person, except the third-person Official bio on /about/.
- **Make the money conversation about value.** Compare the fix with the
  cost of keeping the problem, never with zero. No hourly rates, no
  discounts, no prices on the site.
- **Say no kindly.** "If the honest answer is 'you don't need me,' that's
  what the brief will say." Referring someone elsewhere is part of the
  service.
- **Close with one clear next step.** One action, plainly named.

## Tone by context

The voice stays the same. The tone moves with the moment.

| Context | Tone |
| --- | --- |
| Home, About, FitCheck™ | Warmest. Story, a flipped cliché, a gentle hard truth. |
| Service pages | Most direct. Problem, evidence, outcome, fit, next step. |
| Free tools | Plain and practical. Say what it does and what it can't. |
| Errors and form messages | Calm and specific. What happened, what to do, no blame. |
| Decision Brief and emails | Personal and candid. Answer first, then the reasoning. |

## Rules that don't bend

- **Facts come from Matt.** Never invent results, clients, quotes, dates, or
  numbers. Leave a clear `[gap]` and ask instead.
- **Contractions everywhere.** don't, it's, you're, I'll.
- **No em dashes.** Use a full stop, comma, colon, or parentheses.
- **Name and spelling:** FitCheck™ (one word, capital F and C, with ™).
  Cadence Lab. CX without the BS.
- **Every number has a named source** on the same page.
- **Never quote or imitate a real person's wording.** The reference voices
  shape how we write; the words are ours.

## Words we don't use

leverage, synergy, seamless, unlock, elevate, empower, robust, holistic,
game-changer, cutting-edge, best-in-class, world-class, delve, tapestry,
landscape, journey (as a metaphor for anything except a customer journey),
paradigm, "in today's fast-paced world", "we're passionate about".

Prefer plain, active verbs: fix, find, trace, ship, cut, prove, decide, own.

## Checklist before publishing

1. Does the first sentence carry the point?
2. Is every fact and number Matt's, or sourced on the page?
3. Would a smart, busy reader feel respected, not sold to?
4. Any banned words, em dashes, or missing contractions?
5. Is there exactly one next step?
