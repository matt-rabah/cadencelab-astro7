# FitCheck™ Decision Brief

FitCheck is a free, human-reviewed initial assessment. Matt sends a concise email brief after reviewing the submission. The automated answer receipt is not the brief. No automatic diagnosis, numerical score, delivery deadline, or implementation approval is promised.

## Review method

Review four dimensions: problem clarity (challenge and desired outcome), operating complexity (teams and environment), evidence readiness (data usefulness and examples), and change readiness (sponsor and ability to change work). Mark anything not established by the answers as unknown. A modern system or confident response does not prove readiness.

Choose the most useful first action, explaining competing constraints if necessary:

- **Clarify first:** the affected outcome or scope is unclear. Ask for one concrete example and the desired change.
- **Align first:** the decision spans teams without an accountable owner. Establish the decision owner and participants.
- **Evidence first:** the available information cannot distinguish plausible causes. Identify the smallest useful sample to gather.
- **Stabilize first:** ongoing process changes prevent a meaningful baseline. Define the current workflow and immediate responsibilities first.
- **Ready to diagnose:** there is a material question, an owner, evidence that can be gathered, and authority to act on findings. Explain which diagnostic would answer that question.
- **Explore implementation:** an existing diagnosis, evidence, agreed outcome, and owner may support delivery. Confirm those materials and scope before recommending a build.

Do not select a state mechanically from a single answer. Explain the reported evidence, the inference, and what could change the recommendation. Avoid confidence percentages. If the submission cannot support a recommendation, name the missing information rather than inventing a constraint.

Routing: cross-team customer friction may fit CX Systems Diagnostic; lifecycle and renewal signals may fit Lifecycle Risk Review; CRM data and workflow causes may fit CRM Workflow Audit; a proposed AI use case may fit AI Service Readiness Review. Clear, small problems can route to an existing free tool or internal action. Experience design requires sufficiently established needs and scope. A service recommendation is optional.

## Reply template

Subject: Your FitCheck Decision Brief — [organization]

**Observed problem:** [Paraphrase the reported symptom and desired outcome.]

**Likely problem class and complexity:** [Working interpretation; identify relevant teams/systems and unknowns.]

**Readiness state:** [State and a short reason.]

**Primary constraint:** [Likely barrier, or explain why it is not yet established.]

**Evidence and open questions:** [What supports the interpretation, what is self-reported, and what needs checking.]

**Your next decision:** [One concrete action and what it would help establish.]

**Cadence Lab path, if useful:** [Specific service/resource and why, or no engagement recommended yet.]

This initial brief is based on the information you shared. You can use it whether or not we work together.

## Illustrative example — not a client result

Observed problem: customers repeat information when moving from sales to support. Desired outcome: fewer repeat explanations.

Likely class: cross-team handoff friction. Both teams use the CRM; the submission does not establish which records or rules cause the issue.

State: Align first. The submitter reports no shared decision owner.

Constraint: authority to change the handoff appears unclear; CRM capability has not been assessed.

Next decision: name an owner and trace one recent handoff with sales and support. Record what information was missing and who could change that step.

Path: start internally with the CX Handoff Mapper. Consider a CX Systems Diagnostic if the issue remains unclear after that exercise. Do not recommend automation from these answers alone.
